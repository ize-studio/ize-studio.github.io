Essays

Little by Little

April 27, 2024

Subtitle: Finding My Style

For several days now, I have not been able to touch my fiction. It would be good to write a little every day, but if I read the last part carefully, even when I come back from time to time and continue, the content becomes rich enough. I have always kept some distance from the idea of “pushing forward by my own will and doing something,” and I think that has become one of my styles even in writing. I do not exercise for more than thirty minutes a day, but after doing even that for more than ten years, my body has become fairly good. People who exercise for summer always come to me and ask what I eat and what protein I choose, but I simply tell them, “Do not try to gather information. Pay attention only to what your body wants.” If you do not rush and simply increase the time you spend with exercise, you can stay healthy without overdoing it.

Writing is the same. It took a long time before I made up my mind to write something that would be shown to others. There may be no one who does not have a desire to be recognized through writing, but even so, I sufficiently accepted that I might not be recognized, and still, like exercising alone, I kept writing alone. When I exercise every day, even a little, I can tell what is lacking and what I especially need to pay attention to through sensations like stiffness or itching. Writing is similar. As I write, I come to know what I must write, and what tone I must be careful not to let appear. And when I write while paying attention to those things, writing itself becomes more enjoyable. This is something different from the concept of improvement. Improvement implies small goals and the process of achieving them, but I do not usually set such goals. I am the type who goes slowly, like clothes gradually soaked by drizzle, but without stopping. People who work with me for the first time sometimes find that frustrating. But instead, I do not stop. And at a point not very far along, usually after about two months, when the person who says, as self-help books do, that they are setting goals, managing time, and moving forward productively is exhausted and catching their breath just before burnout, I am still piling up work. That is the moment when the graph of my progress overtakes the graph of the person who sets goals.

Strangely, a person’s style does not seem to be decided by one or two things. For a while, whether in self-help books or when learning work from people around me, I heard a lot of advice like, ask questions while working; seniors do not find questions bothersome; if you take over quickly, won’t the senior feel secure even if they get promoted? But in the end, the answer I could give was this. First, if you ask while working, you receive an answer, but if it ends with merely hearing that answer, then asking was no different from not asking. There is no value in the question. If someone asks, “What happens when you add B to A?” then one must become able to answer, “When B is added to A, it becomes C.” If one says, “So-and-so said that when B is added to A, it becomes C,” then that is not me doing the work. The point is to receive advice while doing the work I need to do, not to ask because I am curious, learn the answer, and decide to memorize it.

Second, when people ask me, I find it bothersome. I teach what must be taught, but I find questions bothersome. There is a reason for that too. It is natural to become curious, but if I think something is necessary, then from the senior’s position I explain it when the time comes. I am not there to satisfy someone’s curiosity. I have a lot of work to do too. So when I receive a question, I ask back why they are asking it. Most people answer that they asked because they could see a menu on the screen. Then my answer is always the same.

“If I tell you what that is, are you going to touch it?”

If it is not needed right now, do not ask. If you do not know what it is, do not touch it carelessly. If you are not going to touch it anyway, do not make me explain for the sake of explanation. Yet there are so many people who ask useless questions while pretending to be diligent.

There may be people for whom those questions are not useless. But among the people who asked me that way, or who asked that way when I was a new employee, one hundred out of one hundred did not remember later what they had asked. I, who had not even asked the question myself, remembered it. That means they had asked while already knowing they would not need it.

I do not mean to boast, but when I asked something, I had a reason. If someone, sounding irritated, said, “That is not something to do at this stage, so why are you asking?” I would answer that I had tried something, that normally it should have gone smoothly, but that this or that situation had occurred, so I had no choice but to ask; if what I had tried was something even a new employee could do, and this or that situation occurred, then wasn’t it naturally something I had to ask about, even as a report? I am the type who, if taught, has to at least try it out. That is why the strength of being slow but steady always shows itself.

Because I built up my writing style that way, I now think a certain style has settled in. Put badly, it means that even if someone dislikes it, I have to write this way. Put well, it means there are that many fewer things to worry about while writing. Since the days of notebooks, I have tried writing this way and that, and this has settled as almost the only method that lets me write without disturbing the flow of my thoughts.

I began using notebooks in 2008. Before that, I wrote as things came on a blog, but I did not write very long pieces. Perhaps it was because my style had not settled. But because I was writing as if summarizing only the core points at once and could not unfold them, that period of writing must still have helped me. Without that experience, I would not have realized the pleasure of writing. I started a blog on Empas around 2003, and that was where I wrote a little more. Cyworld also played some role, but because I thought people I knew would come and read it, I did not really think of writing honestly there.

While writing in notebooks, I first realized that slow writing was not necessarily bad. Because of the time it took my handwriting to finish a sentence, I was not swept away while my thoughts jumped here and there. I could actually follow the branching thoughts. In that way, even though I called it a notebook, it was a kind of journal, and once I began filling a page, I thought writing was enjoyable. From then on, my blog posts also began to grow longer little by little, but there were clear limits. When I wrote in a notebook, the writing became long, but when I wrote on a blog, it did not become very long. Later, while writing in Word files, I came to understand that when the time required to write sentences was too short, it disturbed thought. Only then did I realize the importance of being a little slow.

So if I had pushed forward from the beginning with nothing but the will to write long pieces, would I ever have discovered my style, this need to write at a suitable speed? Perhaps I might have been able to tear even this style apart and still write well. But that is not what I want. I think that if I try to control my body and my mind as I please, I become ill. I probably would simply not have written at all. If I had forced myself to write without finding my own style, I would not have lasted long.
