A Multilayered Decipherment of Arno Schmidt’s “An die Exzellenzen”

Atomic-number substitution, anagrammatic compression, multilingual wordplay, and historical reference

Rupert L. Kim
September 17, 2026

Abstract

Arno Schmidt’s 1949 text “An die Exzellenzen” takes a compressed form in which letters, numbers, abbreviations, punctuation, and fragments from several languages are superimposed. Previous research established several important reference points, including the date of composition, 13.3.49; the connection between GpU and the Soviet GPU; and the link between “18447 g7 gg” and the demolition of Heligoland on April 18, 1947 [1]. This study divides the text into six segments according to its principal punctuation marks and examines how recurring atomic-number→element-symbol→letter-rearrangement operations, local reuse, dates, coordinates, abbreviations, multilingual vocabulary, and historical references overlap. In Segment 1, 8→O, c 357→C’EST, 8xup→COUP by reverse-applying the adjacent-item reuse rule for x identified in Segment 5, and ZEUs→zeuS→Suez combine to restore a French sentence. In Segment 2, the Latin cluster VAE VICTIS and VICTIS / GLADIATORES / PARCUNT / VOS? is joined to Pseudo-Quintilian’s “victis etiam gladiatores parcunt” and to a context concerned with inhuman revenge, restraint, and mercy after victory. Segment 3 yields a Russian rhetorical accusation involving GPU and НЕ УБИЙЦА, while Segment 4 yields the multilingual sequence ZULM, FUSIL, PESHAWAR SE, JIS MEN KHUDAI, HINSA, PEPS, HUBBU, VEG. Segment 5 connects FIUME/1919, SPANIEN–BASKEN–GUERNICA–BOMBENKRIEG, historical reference points in 1899 and 1937, and 10.12.48/SABHI–AFFRE–FLEISCH; its final colon leads directly into Segment 6: i wo! / 18.4.47 / gegen 7 Gg / BEHOBEN. In the trailing phrase, both Glent 31→GELANGT and glent 14 Po→SPION LEGT are obtained by exact letter rearrangement and, together with the author’s name “Arno Schmidt” printed immediately afterward in the original layout, function both as internal deciphering clues and as a terminal device leading into the signature.

Keywords: Arno Schmidt; An die Exzellenzen; literary cipher; atomic number; element symbol; anagram; multilingual compression; AI-assisted analysis

1. Introduction

“An die Exzellenzen” names Truman (Roosevelt), Stalin, and Churchill (Attlee) as its addressees, places Yalta, Tehran, and Potsdam before them, and then presents an abnormal body text mixing letters and numbers. Bruns and Grahl regarded the text as structured and politically meaningful but explicitly stated that they had not achieved a complete decipherment [1]. Even so, they identified several important reference points: the possibility that punctuation divides semantic units; that 13349 represents the date of composition, 13.3.49; that GpU may refer to the Soviet GPU; and that 18447 g7 gg is connected with the demolition of Heligoland on April 18, 1947 [1].

Printed original structure and cipher text:

An die Exzellenzen
Herren
Truman (Roosevelt),
Stalin,
Churchill (Attlee)
Jalta, Teheran, Potsdam

   id 21v18 Pt 7 gallisc 314002a 17 ? V 31 GpU 4a
29, 39, 49 ? mz 71Fi16 34007129 pp 34 udi19jem
13349 bubu WEg !
   Muff 19 exi; 16 enu 070 zIm 4019 abs12c 24 spü, 43
asti siv 13999 idle 48, 19037 pem 8 pho 36. 1012
sabi FR26a FlisCh 26:iwo    18447 g7 gg !
Glent 31, glent 14 Po    Arno Schmidt

This study does not begin by assuming a completed political plaintext. It gives priority to transformations that can be explicitly applied to the actual letter-and-number strings and that recur elsewhere in the text. The decipherment of each segment distinguishes among letter-level transformation, linguistic combination, historical reference points, and sentence-level reconstruction.

Results derived directly from the letters are not treated as having the same evidentiary status as sentences reconstructed through context. Numerical segmentation and anagrams require at least one independent constraint among recurrence elsewhere, internal clues, external historical or textual reference points, and exact letter correspondence.

2. Main Analysis

The main analysis proceeds through the six segments separated by punctuation and then the trailing phrase. Each subsection begins with the relevant original cipher text, followed by an explanation of letter transformation, linguistic and historical background, and sentence-level interpretation.

2.1 Structure and Segmentation of the Cipher Text

Using visible punctuation as semantic boundaries, the body is divided into six segments. “Glent 31, glent 14 Po” occurs in the terminal area after the six segments and visually leads into the author’s name “Arno Schmidt,” so it is treated separately as a trailing phrase.

This segmentation is supported by the coexistence of recurring transformations within individual segments and reuse rules operating across different segments.

The letter-level decipherment in this paper uses as its baseline the transcription of Bargfelder Ausgabe, Werkgruppe III, Band 3, p. 71, reproduced by Bruns and Grahl [1]. In particular, “Muff 19 exi” and “19037,” both important to the decipherment, are preserved exactly as printed in the original; the 0 in “19037” is itself actual character material used in the decipherment and is therefore not omitted.

  1. 8 c 357 8xup ZEUs!
  2. id 21v18 Pt 7 gallisc 314002a 17?
  3. V 31 GpU 4a 29, 39, 49?
  4. mz 71Fi16 34007129 pp 34 udi19jem 13349 bubu WEg!
  5. Muff 19 exi; 16 enu 070 zIm 4019 abs12c 24 spü, 43 asti siv 13999 idle 48, 19037 pem 8 pho 36. 1012 sabi FR26a FlisCh 26:
  6. iwo – 18447 g7 gg!

Trailing phrase: Glent 31, glent 14 Po

2.1.1 Original Text and Rules of Evidence

The following rules are applied to all subsequent decipherment. (1) The baseline cipher text for letter-level analysis is the transcription of Bargfelder Ausgabe, Werkgruppe III, Band 3, p. 71, reproduced by Bruns and Grahl [1]. Other transcriptions are treated only as variants and are not used to generate positive deciphering evidence. (2) All historical identifications are evaluated relative to the date of composition, March 13, 1949. Facts, estimates, terminology, reports, and numerical figures that Schmidt could have encountered by that date are examined first. (3) Modern archival materials and later historical scholarship are recorded separately from information available in 1949 and are used to verify, correct, and contextualize contemporary perceptions. (4) Readings derived directly from the letters are separated from contextual and sentence-level reconstruction. Even a historically plausible sentence is not treated as directly recovered plaintext unless the relevant letters and numbers are accounted for. (5) Numerical segmentation and anagrams require at least one independent constraint among recurrence elsewhere, an internal clue, an external historical or textual reference point, and exact letter correspondence. (6) A single string may simultaneously carry multiple layers—for example, date, atomic number, and vocabulary—but the grounds and function of each layer are presented separately.

2.2 Segment-by-Segment Decipherment

2.2.1 Segment 1: Visual Substitution and a Reuse Marker

Cipher text: 8 c 357 8xup ZEUs!

The initial 8 is read as O according to the atomic-number rule repeated elsewhere (8=O). Applying the visual substitutions 3→E, 5→S, 7→T to c 357 yields C’EST. The x in 8xup is interpreted by reverse-applying the reuse rule obtained from 19x→1919 in Segment 5. x supplies no independent letter; instead, after rearrangement it acts as a marker that recalls the immediately preceding item. Thus the c that has just appeared in c 357→C’EST is reused, and when it is combined with 8=O and up, O+c+u+p can be rearranged into COUP. In the final string ZEUs, not only the letters themselves but also their capitalization is used in the decipherment. On the surface, Z, E, and U are uppercase while only the final s is lowercase: ZEUs. Reversing this capitalization pattern gives zeuS—that is, the first three letters become lowercase and the final one uppercase. Rearranging the resulting z-e-u-S gives S-u-e-z, or Suez, exactly. ZEUs is therefore not treated merely as an irregular spelling of ZEUS; it is interpreted as a construction pointing to Suez through the two stages of capitalization reversal and letter rearrangement.

At the letter level, the transformations are 8→O, c 357→C’EST, 8xup→O+c+u+p→COUP, and ZEUs→zeuS→Suez. The function of x is reverse-applied from the local reuse rule confirmed in Segment 5. ZEUs→zeuS→Suez is a separate two-stage transformation involving capitalization reversal followed by exact rearrangement.

The sentence-level reconstruction therefore proceeds to “Ô, c’est [un] coup [de] Suez!” The function words in brackets are not letters directly recovered from the cipher text but grammatical restoration. The present meaning is taken as, “Oh, it is a strike at Suez!” Within the overall context directed at the Allied leadership, Suez becomes not a secondary anagram hidden behind ZEUS but the principal reading of this segment.

Historical background. In a text written in 1949, SUEZ need not anticipate the Suez Crisis of 1956. The Suez Canal Zone was already a symbol of British imperial and strategic military control. Under the 1936 Anglo-Egyptian Treaty, Britain retained troops and military installations in the Canal Zone, and after the Second World War the Egyptian government strongly demanded withdrawal. A 1946 U.S. diplomatic document records that Egypt’s demand for withdrawal was forceful while Britain sought to retain at least minimal ground and air forces and strategic bases in the Canal Zone [37]. Thus, at the time Schmidt wrote, SUEZ could already carry the immediate political implication of an imperial military order that persisted after the war.

Full decipherment: Ô, c’est [un] coup [de] Suez! — “Oh, a strike at Suez!”

2.2.2 Segment 2: A Latin Accusation Concerning Victors and Vanquished

Cipher text: id 21v18 Pt 7 gallisc 314002a 17?

Segment 2 is analyzed under a stricter criterion of linguistic consistency. Since several items of Classical Latin vocabulary can be recovered in sequence from this segment, the decipherment is kept consistently within the Latin lexical field rather than mixing modern languages.

On that basis, several Latin outputs can be recovered in succession without mixing in modern languages. VAE can be formed from v + a + e, with e supplied by 02=He. VICTIS can be formed from v + i + c + t + i + s. GLADIATORES consists of the letter material G (31=Ga), L (17=Cl), A, D (id), I, A, T (Pt), O (0), R (40=Zr or 18=Ar), E (02=He), and S (21=Sc). PARCUNT consists of P (Pt) + A/R (18=Ar) + C (21=Sc or 17=Cl) + U (the Latin value of v) + N (7=N) + T (Pt). Finally, VOS can be formed from v + O (0) + S. Since other segments already show that numbers and letters can be reused across overlapping outputs, these are treated not as a single-use anagram in which material is consumed only once, but as a network of Latin words sharing letter material.

The most direct textual background is Pseudo-Quintilian’s Declamationes maiores IX.18–19. In the relevant passage, the speaker condemns the cruelty of continuing to take revenge even upon an enemy who has already fallen. He says that even wild beasts pass by one who lies prostrate, offers as higher examples those who spared enemies and restored cities, and then states “victis etiam gladiatores parcunt”: “even gladiators spare the vanquished.” The next sentence asks that merely wishing worse suffering upon an enemy would make one “omnium mortalium inhumanissimum”—the most inhuman of all mortals. Section 19 then says that lasting goodwill is preserved only by “modesta victoria,” restrained victory, and explicitly invokes clementia, mercy. This is therefore more than a lexical coincidence. The rhetorical structure condemning the victor who continues to humiliate and torment the defeated as inhuman, and calling for mercy and restraint, closely overlaps with Schmidt’s accusation addressed to the leaders of the victorious powers [29].

Because the cipher text itself contains no independently identifiable material supplying the m in ETIAM, this study does not claim ETIAM as recovered plaintext. The core directly recoverable from the cipher material is VICTIS / GLADIATORES / PARCUNT together with the interrogative VOS?. The sentence-level restoration therefore marks the supplied word explicitly: “VICTIS [ETIAM] GLADIATORES PARCUNT — VOS?”, or “Even gladiators spare the vanquished — and you?” The same letter material also yields another complete Latin expression, VAE VICTIS. GALLIS is directly connected with the classical scene in Livy, Ab urbe condita 5.48, where the Gauls bring unfair weights and Brennus places even his sword on the scales before declaring “vae victis,” “woe to the vanquished” [30]. The two citations share VICTIS as their pivot. One voices the brutal logic of the victor; the other states the counter-principle that even gladiators show mercy to the defeated.

Two passages from Caesar reinforce the same accusation concerning victor and vanquished. In De bello Gallico 1.44, Ariovistus says “quod victores victis imponere consuerint,” justifying tribute on the ground that victors customarily impose burdens on the defeated [31]. Book 7, chapter 28 records that after the fall of Avaricum the Romans “non aetate confectis, non mulieribus, non infantibus pepercerunt”—spared neither the aged, nor women, nor infants [32]. These passages further support the convergence of GALLIS, VICTIS, PARCUNT, and the vocabulary of mercy and inhumanity within a single Classical Latin semantic field. The earlier path treating 17, 18, and 21 primarily as coordinate references to Caesar’s chapter numbers is therefore no longer the core mechanism. The stronger result is that within Latin itself, the rhetoric of victor and vanquished, mercy and cruelty, converges directly.

The sentence-level reconstruction of Segment 2 is “VAE VICTIS? VICTIS [ETIAM] GLADIATORES PARCUNT — VOS?” In English: “Woe to the vanquished? Even gladiators spare the vanquished — and you?” The point is not a neutral comparison of territorial division but the treatment shown by victors toward the defeated. The final question mark functions as a moral rhetorical question, turning the classical language of victory and mercy back upon the victorious powers addressed by the text.

Literary and historical background. Livy 5.48’s vae victis (“woe to the vanquished”) is associated with the story of the Gallic leader Brennus and the Roman ransom. When the Romans protest that the Gauls’ weights are unfair, Brennus places his sword on the scales as well and utters the phrase [30]. Pseudo-Quintilian, Declamationes maiores IX.18–19, supplies a directly opposed proposition that fits this segment even more closely. “Victis etiam gladiatores parcunt”—“even gladiators spare the vanquished”—is followed immediately by a rebuke of extreme inhumanity, and the next section speaks of modesta victoria and clementia: restrained victory and mercy [29]. Caesar’s De bello Gallico 1.44 contains the victores victis logic of what victors impose upon the defeated, while 7.28 records the failure to spare even the elderly, women, and children at Avaricum [31][32]. The Latin cluster in Segment 2 therefore forms not merely a set of classical citations but a textual network concentrating an ethical question: “How are the defeated treated after victory?”

Full decipherment: VAE VICTIS? VICTIS [ETIAM] GLADIATORES PARCUNT — VOS? — “Woe to the vanquished? Even gladiators spare the vanquished — and you?”

2.2.3 Segment 3: GPU and a Russian Accusation

Cipher text: V 31 GpU 4a 29, 39, 49?

In Segment 3, GpU is independently the strongest-supported element. Bruns and Grahl likewise connect it with the Soviet GPU [1]. The surrounding 29, 39, and 49 are read as atomic-number inputs.

Historical background: what was the GPU? GPU is the abbreviation of the Russian Государственное политическое управление (Gosudarstvennoe politicheskoe upravlenie), the State Political Directorate. It was the Soviet political-police organization that succeeded the Cheka in 1922, and in 1923 it was reorganized at the Union level as the OGPU (Объединённое государственное политическое управление, the Unified State Political Directorate). Thus, strictly speaking, the official institutional name in 1931 was OGPU rather than GPU. The cipher’s GpU is therefore more historically accurate if understood not as the exact official designation of a 1931 office but as a compressed reference to the GPU family of Soviet political-police organizations. The Library of Congress guide to the Russian archives likewise explains the organizational lineage from the Cheka to the GPU and the functions of the political police [38].

The year 1931 is also concrete. During Stalin’s forced collectivization and so-called “dekulakization,” the OGPU was deeply involved in mass deportation, the surveillance and administration of special settlements, and the supply of forced labor. A study of the Kuzbass notes that the OGPU supplied forced labor beginning in 1930 and that, as the special-settlement system was organized nationally in 1931, settlers were placed under OGPU supervision and could not leave the settlements without permission [39]. The reconstruction В 31 ГПУ НЕ УБИЙЦА? (“Was the GPU in 1931 not a murderer?” / “You mean the GPU of 1931 was not a murderer?”) can therefore be read not as a sentence tossing out an obscure abbreviation but as an accusatory rhetorical question directly invoking the apparatus of coercion, deportation, and political policing under the early Stalinist regime.

“4a 29, 39, 49” can be converted as 4=Be, the original a, 29=Cu, 39=Y, and 49=In. The resulting letters BEACUYIN can be rearranged, without addition or deletion, into NE UBIYCA. Read as the Latin-script skeleton of Russian, this corresponds to the Cyrillic НЕ УБИЙЦА (“not a murderer” / “is not a murderer”).

The initial V is read as the Russian preposition в (“in/at”), 31 as 1931, and GpU as ГПУ. Segment 3 as a whole is therefore reconstructed as the compressed Russian rhetorical question В 31 ГПУ НЕ УБИЙЦА? (“Was the GPU in 1931 not a murderer?”). Including the final question mark, it reads less like a neutral question about institutional nomenclature than a sarcastic accusatory question about the GPU’s violence.

At the letter level, the most direct transformation is 4a 29, 39, 49→BEACUYIN→NE UBIYCA. The step from there to Cyrillic НЕ УБИЙЦА, together with V→в and 31→1931, belongs to the linguistic-combination level. The cipher uses the compressed marker GpU even though the official institutional name in 1931 was OGPU.

Full decipherment: В 31 ГПУ НЕ УБИЙЦА? — “Was the GPU in 1931 not a murderer?”

2.2.4 Segment 4: Peshawar and Overlapping Multilingual Compression

Cipher text: mz 71Fi16 34007129 pp 34 udi19jem 13349 bubu WEg!

The first overlap is mz+71. Atomic number 71 is Lu, and the letter material m-z-Lu rearranges exactly into ZULM. Zulm means oppression, injustice, or tyranny in Hindustani/Urdu usage. The same 71 is reused in 71Fi16. Since 71=Lu and 16=S, Lu+Fi+S rearranges exactly into FUSIL, French for “rifle.” The reuse of a single number in different lexical outputs illustrates the compressed method operating in this segment. Atomic numbers follow the standard periodic table [5]. FUSIL is also confirmed by Larousse [12].

34007129 can be read as approximately 34°00′N, 71°29′E, pointing to the Peshawar region. The immediately following 34 supplies the element symbol Se. In Hindustani, se functions as a postposition with instrumental and ablative senses such as “by,” “from,” or “with.” The coordinate string together with the following 34 can therefore be read as PESHAWAR SE, “from Peshawar/by Peshawar.” This coordinate reading becomes more specific and more strongly supported through its connection with the adjacent multilingual sequence.

Historical background: Peshawar in 1930. The coordinates point to Peshawar in the North-West Frontier Province of British India. On April 23, 1930, during the Civil Disobedience Movement, a crowd gathered in Qissa Khwani (“Storytellers”) Bazaar after leaders associated with Abdul Ghaffar Khan had been arrested. The Khudai Khidmatgar (“Servants of God”) was an explicitly nonviolent anti-colonial movement composed largely of Pashtuns. Troops and armored vehicles under British command were deployed and fired into the crowd in the narrow market, causing many casualties. Because death tolls vary substantially between contemporary records and later research, this paper does not fix a single number. A Cambridge study emphasizes the unarmed and nonviolent character of the protesters, the harsh colonial repression, and the refusal by two platoons of the Garhwali regiment to obey orders to fire on the crowd [40]. British Library material also preserves photographic records of Qissa Khwani Bazaar and British military control there on April 23, 1930 [42].

This background adds a highly specific second layer to KHUDAI. In the lexical path below, khudai is read in the sense of digging, excavation, or laying bare and connected with the German freilegen (“to uncover/reveal”). Yet KHUDAI immediately beside the place-name Peshawar also matches the first word of the actual movement name Khudai Khidmatgar. The very fact that PESHAWAR and KHUDAI occur within the same local string therefore connects directly with the 1930 Qissa Khwani event. This proper-name layer does not replace the existing lexical layer; it is recorded as an additional, historically independent correspondence.

13349 can be segmented as 1 33 49→H As In, and HAsIn rearranges exactly into HINSA without addition or deletion. Hinsa denotes violence or harm in Hindustani/Sanskritic usage. The date reading 13.3.49 proposed in previous scholarship is retained as a separate date layer [1]. The HAsIn letter material can also overlap with the neighboring string. Applying 19=K in udi19jem and combining it with HAsIn yields letter material that rearranges exactly into JIS MEN KHUDAI.

JIS MEN KHUDAI provides a decisive connection at the semantic level. Jis men means “in which / in that which / within.” Romanized khudai is polysemous: one semantic family concerns divinity or divine power, while another concerns digging, excavation, or breaking open. At the sentence level, the latter sense is adopted, with German freilegen covering both the physical sense of uncovering something buried and the abstract sense of exposing or revealing something hidden. pp overlaps with the immediately following 34=Se material and rearranges exactly as pp+Se→PPSE→PEPS; the French peps, meaning vigor, energy, or vitality, reinforces the semantic field of force or drive in VEG. bubu overlaps with H from neighboring HAsIn and rearranges exactly as bubu+H→HUBBU; the hubb/hubbu semantic field of love, attachment, and desire supplies the sentence-level slot “obsession.” Finally, WEg connects to VEG through the German pronunciation of W as /v/. This sequence leads to the sentence-level interpretation “die durch die Gewehrgewalt in Peschawar freigelegte Besessenheit von Macht und Gewalt,” that is, “the obsession with power and violence laid bare by the rifle repression in Peshawar.”

Full decipherment: die durch die Gewehrgewalt in Peschawar freigelegte Besessenheit von Macht und Gewalt. — “The obsession with power and violence laid bare by the rifle repression in Peshawar.”

2.2.5 Segment 5: An Accusation of Historical Violence and the Postwar Order

Cipher text: Muff 19 exi; 16 enu 070 zIm 4019 abs12c 24 spü, 43 asti siv 13999 idle 48, 19037 pem 8 pho 36. 1012 sabi FR26a FlisCh 26:

Control of historical knowledge. Since this text was written on March 13, 1949, historical identifications in this study are evaluated on two levels. First, information, estimates, terminology, and published reports that Schmidt could have encountered by that date are checked. Second, modern historical research and archival materials are used to assess how contemporary beliefs differ from later-established facts. A decipherment candidate is not automatically rejected merely because a figure accessible in 1949 differs from a modern corrected figure. The historically important question is what numbers and facts Schmidt himself could actually have encoded. Modern knowledge is not used anachronistically to replace the author’s information environment but as supplementary material for verification and interpretation.

The opening “Muff 19 exi” can be read as an overlapping FIUME / 1919 structure. x supplies no independent letter; after rearrangement it acts as a marker recalling the immediately preceding item. Here, the 19 immediately before x is reused, giving 19 19→1919. Reverse-applying this rule to Segment 1 lets the c that has just appeared in c 357→C’EST be reused in 8xup to produce COUP. At the same time, Muff+e+i supplies the letters of FIUME while leaving one additional f. This extra f is not discarded. Since F is the sixth letter of the Latin alphabet, a secondary date layer F→6→June can be read beside FIUME / 1919. The Fiume question was one of the major territorial disputes directly handled by Wilson, Lloyd George, Clemenceau, and Orlando at the Paris Peace Conference in 1919 [20], and the Council of Four again discussed the Fiume issue on June 26 and June 28, 1919 [23].

The next cluster, “16 enu 070 zIm 4019 abs12c 24 spü,” becomes structurally clearer if atomic-number substitution and overlapping reuse are allowed. From it, the lexical field SPANIEN – BASKEN – GUERNICA – BOMBENKRIEG is repeatedly recovered. The process uses 16=S, 7=N, 40=Zr, 19=K, 12=Mg, 24=Cr, together with the visible letters, while allowing one item of letter material to participate in multiple outputs, as already observed in Segment 4. This is not a claim that the entire string forms a single anagram whose letters are consumed once; it is a chain of overlapping extractions from the same compressed material. The convergence is historically coherent as well. Guernica was a Basque town bombed during the Spanish Civil War in April 1937 and later became a symbol of wartime violence against civilians [21].

Historical background: Spain, the Basques, and Guernica. On April 26, 1937, during the Spanish Civil War, the Basque town of Gernika/Guernica was bombed by German and Italian air forces supporting Franco’s Nationalists. The attack became an international symbol of civilian vulnerability in modern aerial warfare and directly prompted Picasso’s Guernica [21]. Bilbao, the principal Basque city, fell to Nationalist forces on June 19, 1937 [24], while Picasso’s large Guernica canvas was completed in early June and related studies continued through the same month [25]. SPANIEN–BASKEN–GUERNICA–BOMBENKRIEG therefore forms a historically cohesive cluster of violence. This paper does not recast the perpetrators of the Guernica bombing as the Allies. The event functions within a list of European wartime violence set against the humanitarian postwar order later proclaimed by the victorious powers.

In the latter part of the string, the following transformations are obtained: 43+asti+siv→Tc+asti+siv→ACTIVISTS; 13999→13 9 99→Al F Es→FALSE; idle+48(Cd)→DEL CID; pem+8(O)→POEM; and, splitting 36 as 3 6→Li C, pho+36→PHILCO. These results are recorded with a distinction between historical reference points that enter directly into the main chain of accusation and auxiliary lexical outputs mechanically recoverable from the same compressed material.

The exact reading 13999→FALSE remains central to Segment 5. The same string also permits the date reading 13.9.1899 considered in previous research [1]. The current reconstruction does not force the date layer and lexical layer to compete; both are retained. Historical research quoting a contemporary Lansdowne document records that on September 13, 1899, he judged 48,000 men sufficient for war with the Transvaal [7]. This supplies a reference point to British imperial warfare within the same accusatory list, while FALSE operates beside it as an overtly ironic or condemnatory word.

Following Bruns and Grahl, 19037 is retained as a compressed notation for 1937 [1]. Within that framework, the adjacent 8 and 36 continue to connect with contemporary reports of the Soviet purges. Reports from 1937 describe the trial and execution of eight senior military leaders and the later execution of thirty-six Siberian railway workers [8][13]. This historical reference point is consistent with the overall accusatory direction toward Stalin as one of the addressees, while remaining a layer separate from exact letter transformation.

Historical background: the Soviet purges of 1937. In June 1937, Marshal Mikhail Tukhachevsky and seven other senior Soviet military commanders were executed after a secret trial, and foreign reports at the time presented these “eight men” as a single shocking event [8][17]. Later reports that same year also state that thirty-six railway workers in Siberia were executed [13]. Thus 8 and 36 are not abstract numbers but compressed numerical markers of Soviet political violence that a reader in 1937 could actually have encountered in newspapers and magazines.

In the final cluster, the irony becomes more explicit. 1012 is read as December 10, 1948, the date on which the Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted [9]. At the same time, overlapping the H supplied by 1 with sabi yields SABHI (Hindi sabhī, “all, everyone”) [22]. The following FR26a rearranges exactly as FR+Fe+a→AFFRE, while FlisCh 26 receives e from Fe to form FLEISCH, with one F overlapping in reuse [15][16]. The ending is therefore read not as praise of the Declaration but as an accusation through contrast. Rights are proclaimed for “all,” while the historical record just enumerated is filled with terror and slaughter.

When the extra F is compared with other historical anchors in Segment 5, a June layer repeatedly appears. On June 26, 1919, Wilson, Lloyd George, and Clemenceau discussed the Fiume situation, and on June 28 they again discussed Italy’s control of Fiume and the authority involved in demands for withdrawal [23]. In the Spain/Basque chain, Bilbao fell to Nationalist forces on June 19, 1937 [24]. Picasso’s Guernica itself is dated May 11–June 4, 1937, and Reina Sofía materials preserve related studies or follow-up works dated June 3, 4, 8, 19, 22, and 26 [25]. For the 1899 British–Transvaal reference point, the Bloemfontein Conference between Kruger and Milner began on May 30 and ended without agreement on June 5 [26]. For the 1937 Soviet-purge reference point, the June 21 issue of TIME reported the trial of Tukhachevsky and seven other top military officers, eight in all, while a July 5 follow-up described the preceding week’s purge developments and reported that thirty-six additional “wreckers” had been executed at Khabarovsk [27]. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was ultimately adopted on December 10, 1948, while the third session of the Commission on Human Rights ran from May 24 to June 18 and its report, including the draft declaration, was issued on June 28 [28]. The F that at first appears surplus or overlapping in Segment 5 is therefore not simply discarded; it is recorded as a secondary 6/June date layer based on F’s position as the sixth letter of the alphabet.

The principal chain in Segment 5 is therefore FIUME 1919 [F→6→June as a secondary date layer]; SPANIEN – BASKEN – GUERNICA – BOMBENKRIEG; FALSE / 13.9.1899; 1937 and the purge numbers; 10.12.48 – SABHI – AFFRE – FLEISCH. The colon after FlisCh 26 is read not as a terminal mark but as punctuation leading directly into Segment 6. The natural-language reconstruction of Segments 5 and 6 together is given after the decipherment of Segment 6.

Full decipherment: Fiume 1919; Spanien, die Basken, Guernica, Bombenkrieg; die Säuberungen von 1937 — und dann, am 10. Dezember 1948, die Rechte aller Menschen: — “Fiume 1919; Spain and the Basques, Guernica and the bombing war; the purges of 1937 — and then, on December 10, 1948, ‘the rights of all human beings’:”

2.2.6 Segment 6: The Rebuttal Continuing from Segment 5

Cipher text: iwo – 18447 g7 gg!

Segment 6 is read as a direct response to the colon ending Segment 5. The initial string iwo is the German interjection “i wo!” compressed without an internal space; Duden gives the colloquial meaning keineswegs, that is, “not at all,” “nonsense,” or “no way” [33]. Because it comes immediately after Segment 5’s 10.12.48 / SABHI (“all”) / AFFRE / FLEISCH and the colon, i wo! functions as an immediate rebuttal: “Rights for all human beings? Nonsense!” The omission of the internal space is consistent with the compressed character of the cipher as a whole, and the exclamation mark fits this forceful negative function.

The number string 18447 simultaneously carries two layers. First, as a date it reads 18.4.47, April 18, 1947. On that day Britain carried out a massive demolition on Heligoland, and Heligoland’s official historical material gives the total explosive mass as approximately 6,700 tons [36]. Converted to SI mass, 6,700 tons = 6.7×10^9 g = 6.7 Gg, approximately 7 Gg. The following g7 gg can be normalized as “g 7 Gg.” The first g, appearing before a number, is read as an abbreviation of German gegen in its “approximately/about” sense [34], while the final gg is restored to the proper SI capitalization Gg. Thus g7 gg yields “gegen 7 Gg,” approximately 7 Gg. Bruns and Grahl had already identified the connection among 18.4.47, Heligoland, and the quantity of explosives [1]; the present reading also accounts for the remaining initial g within the sentence.

Historical background: Heligoland at the end of the war and after it. Heligoland had been used as a German naval base. On April 18, 1945, shortly before the end of the war in Europe, the Royal Air Force heavily bombed the island. Heligoland’s official historical material records that around 1,000 British aircraft dropped roughly 7,000 bombs over 104 minutes, rendering the island uninhabitable and forcing the residents to evacuate the next day. Exactly two years later, on April 18, 1947, British authorities carried out a massive demolition of the remaining military installations. About 4,000 torpedo warheads, around 9,000 depth charges, more than 91,000 artillery shells, and other munitions—about 6,700 tons of explosives in total—were used; it was the largest non-nuclear explosion carried out up to that time [36]. The blast did not erase the island entirely, but it destroyed major military installations and greatly altered the southern terrain.

This chronology is important to the connected reading of Segments 5 and 6. The 1947 explosion was not a wartime combat action but a demolition of military installations carried out by the British occupation authorities after the war, when the residents had already left the island. The paper therefore reads the event as a concrete postwar case placed immediately after the universal language of human rights dated December 10, 1948. This is an interpretation of the cipher’s rhetorical structure; the date, the British responsibility for the demolition, and the approximately 6,700 tons of explosives are independently confirmed by historical material [36].

Second, 18447 also fits exactly into the repeatedly observed atomic-number transformation. Splitting it as 1 8 4 4 7 gives H O Be Be N, producing the letters HOBEBEN. Rearranging them without addition or deletion yields BEHOBEN. Behoben is the past participle of beheben; Duden gives the relevant sense as removing, eliminating, or remedying something bad, a problem, or a defect [35]. In the Heligoland context the word creates an intentionally chilling double meaning: the same number that marks the date of the demolition simultaneously produces a word meaning “dealt with / eliminated / remedied.” Date, explosive mass, and BEHOBEN are therefore not three unrelated guesses but three layers compressed into the same short string.

Each string in Segment 6 is deciphered individually, but when read together with Segment 5 the meaning of the larger sentence is completed. The connected reconstruction is: “Fiume 1919; Spanien, die Basken, Guernica, Bombenkrieg; die Säuberungen von 1937 — und dann, am 10. Dezember 1948, die Rechte aller Menschen: i wo! Am 18. April 1947 hatte man Helgoland mit gegen 7 Gg bereits ‘behoben’!” In English: “Fiume 1919; Spain and the Basques, Guernica and the bombing war; the purges of 1937 — and then, on December 10, 1948, ‘the rights of all human beings’? Nonsense! By April 18, 1947, Heligoland had already been ‘dealt with’ using about 7 gigagrams of explosives!” The colon at the end of Segment 5 passes 10.12.48 / SABHI / AFFRE / FLEISCH directly into i wo!, and 18.4.47 / gegen 7 Gg / BEHOBEN supplies Heligoland as the concrete postwar case.

Full decipherment: i wo! Am 18. April 1947 hatte man Helgoland mit gegen 7 Gg bereits „behoben“! — “Nonsense! By April 18, 1947, Heligoland had already been ‘dealt with’ using about 7 Gg of explosives!”

2.2.7 Trailing Phrase: Internal Deciphering Clue and Signature

Cipher text: Glent 31, glent 14 Po

Atomic number 31 is Ga. Replacing 31 with Ga in “Glent 31” gives GLENTGA, which rearranges without residue into GELANGT. 31=Ga is the standard periodic-table value [5], and GELANGT is an actual inflected form of the German verb gelangen [11].

This result provides internal grounds for taking atomic-number substitution seriously. Without such a clue, segmenting 13349 or 13999 into atomic numbers could be criticized as an opportunistic, post hoc division of digits. The trailing phrase suggests that the operation number→element symbol→rearrangement may be built into the text itself.

The second phrase is solved exactly in the same way. Since 14=Si, glent 14 Po becomes glent+Si+Po, and the complete letter string GLENTSiPo rearranges, without addition or deletion, into SPION LEGT. Spion means “spy,” and legt, the third-person singular of legen, means “places/puts.” Isolated from its context, the phrase lacks an object; in the original layout, however, the trailing phrase occupies the terminal position of the document, with the author’s name “Arno Schmidt” placed immediately in the same terminal field [2]. GELANGT can therefore be supplemented by the page position as “[bis hierher] gelangt,” “has reached [this point],” while SPION LEGT can be read as a visual pun taking the adjacent author’s name as though it were its object. Expanded into natural language, the terminal device becomes: “[Reached this point]; the spy places — Arno Schmidt.”

Full decipherment: [Bis hierher] gelangt; Spion legt — Arno Schmidt. — “[Reached this point]; the spy places — Arno Schmidt.”

2.3 Integration of the Decipherment Results

2.3.1 From Individual Devices to a Shared Transformation System

The initial working hypothesis was that the six punctuation-delimited segments might each employ a different cipher. This hypothesis was useful because it prevented an early commitment to a single universal substitution rule. Segment 1 uses visual substitution, a typographic meta-marker, and capitalization-guided rearrangement; Segment 2 adds selective extraction from element symbols; Segment 3 combines abbreviations, atomic numbers, anagramming, and conversion into Russian Cyrillic; Segment 6 uses a date and a scientific unit. At that stage, the diversity of devices appeared to be the principal structure.

The model changed when atomic-number operations appeared in Segment 4 and then again in Segment 5 and the trailing phrase. The recurrence was not merely thematic. The same explicit operation—replacing a number with its element symbol and rearranging the resulting letters—produced words at widely separated positions. The trailing phrase may even provide an internal demonstration of the operation itself.

The current model is therefore neither “one cipher per segment” nor “one universal cipher governing the entire text.” It is a repertoire model in which different local devices coexist: visual substitution, atomic-number substitution, element symbols, letter rearrangement, dates, abbreviations, multilingual forms, historical references, and scientific notation overlap in use.

This model also changes the significance of ambiguity. In ordinary decipherment, two readings of the same numerical string compete with one another. Here, 13349 and 13999 suggest that multiple readings themselves may be intentional. One number can function as a date while simultaneously serving as atomic-number material for a separate lexical layer.

2.3.2 Comparison with Previous Research

At an early stage of repeated exploration, the work proceeded from an overly strong impression that the overall meaning of “An die Exzellenzen” had scarcely been uncovered. Direct comparison with Bruns and Grahl required that assumption to be corrected. Their 2014 study explicitly states that it did not reach a complete decipherment, but it identifies the political direction of the text as an accusation addressed to the Allies, recognizes that punctuation divides sentence-like semantic units, and concludes that the text is not a classical one-to-one substitution cipher but a kind of Kurzschrift or shorthand—that is, compressed linguistic content rather than encryption in the classical cryptographic sense [1].

Results already present in previous research include the semantic-unit function of punctuation, the reading of 13349 as the date of composition, the connection between GpU and the Soviet GPU, the connection between 18447 g7 gg and the Heligoland demolition, the possibility that 13999 and 19037 are date-like expressions, and the observation that exi, siv, and idle may be Latin or other foreign-language material [1]. These points are not claimed here as new discoveries.

The contribution proposed by the present analysis is narrower and more specific. It adds a recurring element-symbol/anagram mechanism to the established reference points and demonstrates candidate applications at separated locations. Central examples are mz+71(Lu)→ZULM; 71Fi16→FUSIL; 13349→1 33 49→H As In→HINSA; 13999→FALSE; Glent 31→GELANGT; the Segment 2 Latin cluster GALLIS / VAE VICTIS / VICTIS–GLADIATORES–PARCUNT / VOS?; and Segment 3’s 4a 29,39,49→BEACUYIN→NE UBIYCA→НЕ УБИЙЦА (“not a murderer”). Segment 4 extends the chain through 34007129→PESHAWAR, 34→Se, udi19jem+HAsIn→JIS MEN KHUDAI, pp+Se→PEPS, bubu+H→HUBBU, and WEg→VEG. Segment 5 adds FIUME/1919 through the reuse marker x, the overlapping lexical field SPANIEN–BASKEN–GUERNICA–BOMBENKRIEG, and the 10.12.48/SABHI–AFFRE–FLEISCH sequence, after which the final colon leads directly into Segment 6. Segment 6’s i wo! negates the preceding universal claim and is immediately followed by the Heligoland counterexample 18.4.47 / about 7 Gg / BEHOBEN. The value of the proposal lies in recurrence, internal evidence, explicit letter correspondence, and the distinction between exact transformations and sentence-level synthesis.

The relationship to previous research is therefore cumulative rather than adversarial. Historical reference points established by earlier scholars constrain the search space, and the newly proposed operations attempt to explain additional local structures without discarding those earlier points.

2.4 Verification and Interpretation

2.4.1 Distinguishing Strength of Evidence

The results are divided into three levels. The first consists of readings with external reference points: reading 13349 as the date of composition 13.3.49; reading GpU as the Soviet GPU; and reading 18447 g7 gg as the Heligoland event. These are supported independently of the new element-symbol hypothesis.

The second consists of mechanically reproducible candidate transformations. These include mz+71(Lu)→ZULM; 71Fi16→FUSIL; 13349→1 33 49→H As In→HINSA; 13999→FALSE; Glent 31→GELANGT; the Segment 2 outputs VAE, VICTIS, GLADIATORES, PARCUNT, and VOS from overlapping letter material; and Segment 3’s 4a 29,39,49→BEACUYIN→NE UBIYCA. Segment 4 also contains exact cross-string rearrangements: udi19jem+HAsIn→JIS MEN KHUDAI using 19=K, pp+Se→PEPS, and bubu+H→HUBBU. WEg→VEG is a phonetic link based on the German /v/ value of W. The Peshawar coordinate reading and the sentence-level German synthesis belong to a higher interpretive layer and are not equated with letter-level transformation.

The third consists of contextual or linguistic-level reconstruction. This includes the proposed complete sentence, the expansion of Segment 3 as В 31 ГПУ НЕ УБИЙЦА? (“Was the GPU in 1931 not a murderer?”), the sentence-level interpretation of mechanically explained Segment 6, the interpretation connecting Segment 2’s Latin cluster with Pseudo-Quintilian IX.18–19, Livy 5.48, and classical rhetoric concerning victor and vanquished, and the historical narrative organized around 1899, 1937, and 1948. Segment 5’s pem+8→POEM and pho+36→PHILCO are classified as auxiliary overlapping outputs. The main accusatory sentence is controlled by the FIUME/1919, GUERNICA/BOMBENKRIEG, FALSE, purge, and Universal Declaration/AFFRE/FLEISCH reference points.

Maintaining this distinction prevents the most natural-sounding sentence from automatically becoming the strongest result. At the same time, weaker hypotheses can be preserved as targets for later verification rather than discarded.

2.4.2 Methodological Constraints and Falsifiability

Numerical segmentation and anagramming are inherently vulnerable to overfitting. If digits can be divided arbitrarily and letters rearranged without restriction, accidental words are easy to produce. Additional constraints are therefore required before a candidate can be elevated to a strong result.

Five criteria are particularly important. First, the same transformation should recur in different locations. Second, where possible, the text itself should contain a clue suggesting the operation. Third, independent historical or textual reference points should constrain interpretation whenever available. Fourth, exact letter correspondence is preferred to methods requiring unexplained addition, deletion, or residue. Fifth, every historical claim must carry a time marker. For a text dated March 13, 1949, the first question is whether the relevant fact, estimate, term, or reported number was actually accessible by that date. Modern archival knowledge is recorded separately so that both contemporary information and later correction remain visible within the evidentiary process.

The element-symbol repertoire is now supported by recoveries from structurally different locations. Glent 31→GELANGT offers a candidate internal clue for atomic-number substitution. Segment 4 reuses 71 in mz+Lu→ZULM and Lu+Fi+S→FUSIL; 13349 yields H As In→HINSA and shares the HAsIn material with udi19jem to produce JIS MEN KHUDAI. It then shares Se with pp to produce PEPS and H with bubu to produce HUBBU. Segment 5 independently produces 13999→Al F Es→FALSE. Segment 2 uses Sc/Ar/Ga/Zr/He/Cl together with visible letters in overlapping fashion to produce the Latin cluster VAE / VICTIS / GLADIATORES / PARCUNT / VOS, and Segment 3’s 4a 29,39,49→BEACUYIN→NE UBIYCA extends the same number-to-element repertoire. Because of these mutually reinforcing constraints, the multilayered model is stronger than a collection of unrelated anagrams.

The model is falsifiable in a practical sense. If the proposed numerical segmentations do not match the authoritative original text, if unexplained addition or deletion of letters is repeatedly required, or if a simpler rule explains more strings, the current multilayered model should be weakened. Conversely, the more often the same operation recurs independently in separated locations and converges with historical and textual reference points, the greater the model’s explanatory strength.

Control of textual variants is also necessary. During exploration, one secondary transcription treated the middle character of 19037 as a letter, producing a path to KORB through element-symbol rearrangement. Rechecking the textually faithful Bargfeld transcription showed that the character was the numeral 0. The KORB path is therefore abandoned and is not used as evidence in this paper.

2.4.3 Interpretation: Multilayered Literary Compression

The structure now visible is best understood as a concretization of the Kurzschrift model proposed by Bruns and Grahl [1]. They argued that the text is not encrypted in the classical sense but is compressed linguistic notation. The present analysis proposes candidate mechanisms for some of that compression. Numbers can function as dates, atomic numbers, quantities, and historical coordinates; element symbols become letter material; capitalization can select specific letters or itself act as a deciphering marker; typographic signs can function as meta-markers; letters can be rearranged not only across several languages but, through transliteration, across writing systems; and abbreviations and historical events constrain semantic fields. This concrete operating model is termed “multilayered literary compression.”

Under this model, the failure of a search for one global substitution table becomes understandable. Regularity lies not in a fixed one-to-one key but in a repertoire of transformations. The reader must identify which operation is activated locally and when multiple operations are intentionally superimposed.

The multilayered character of 13349 illustrates this structure clearly. The number preserves the independently established date of composition, 13.3.49, while segmentation as 1 33 49 gives H As In, which rearranges into HINSA. The separate number string 13999 independently produces FALSE while also allowing the date reading 13.9.1899. The trailing phrase directly demonstrates the same family of transformations twice: Glent 31→GELANGT and glent 14 Po→SPION LEGT are both obtained exactly, and the fact that the phrase is printed immediately before the author’s name “Arno Schmidt” shows that the second result can also function as a terminal device leading into the signature.

On this reading, ambiguity may not be merely noise left by an incomplete decipherment. It may be part of the design itself. A single short string can simultaneously compress historical reference, lexical material, and a deciphering instruction. A complete decipherment would therefore have to reconstruct not only one plaintext but also the structure of these overlapping readings.

3. Conclusion

The core of the analysis is that atomic-number substitution, manipulation of element symbols, letter rearrangement, local reuse, dates, coordinates, scientific units, and multilingual vocabulary are used in overlapping fashion. This repertoire produces ZULM, FUSIL, HINSA, FALSE, GELANGT, and SPION LEGT; in Segment 2 it forms a Latin accusation concerning victor and vanquished; in Segment 3, a Russian rhetorical accusation aimed at the GPU; in Segment 4, the multilingual Peshawar chain; and in Segments 5 and 6, a list of historical violence followed by the i wo!–Heligoland rebuttal to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Read as a whole, the text becomes an accusation presenting the postwar order and violence that continued after victory back to the leaders of the victorious powers.

All strings in the six segments and the trailing phrase acquire an interpretive role within the transformation system proposed here. Because exact letter-level transformations and sentences reconstructed through historical and linguistic context differ in evidentiary strength, the paper presents these layers separately.

Including the trailing phrase, both Glent 31→GELANGT and glent 14 Po→SPION LEGT once again demonstrate the rule atomic number→element symbol→rearrangement. In particular, SPION LEGT and the author’s name “Arno Schmidt,” printed in the same terminal area, superimpose a demonstration of the deciphering rule and a signature device within a single ending.
