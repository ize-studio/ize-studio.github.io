The Hobby of Writing

April 27, 2024

At first glance, people who talk a lot seem as if they would have plenty to write if they simply gathered half of what they say. In English conversation, talkative people often learn and practice expressions quickly because they want to say the same things in English. English is not mastered merely by study; it must be absorbed steadily, with less greed and less haste. Still, the thought makes sense. In conversation practice, someone gives you topics and expressions and makes you speak about them. In my case, I do not even talk about such topics with others in Korean, so the quality of practice feels different from that of people who merely have to translate what they already say. Assembling the necessary words to deliver meaning is the same in English and Korean.

But writing is another matter. For me, writing is an intensely private domain. I dislike even showing others the act of writing, and I do not show what I write to people I know. Posting online is different. That is after the writing has been shaped. If conversation does not work for me because it cannot remain personal, writing works because it can take place in a personal dimension. A talkative person does not speak without an interlocutor. And perhaps those who mutter to themselves often do not talk that much with others, while those who talk a lot with others do not necessarily mutter nonstop when alone.

Then is writing the same as talking to oneself? Not quite. I do not like talking to myself. Sometimes I fall into the habit, but once I notice it, I close my mouth and consciously stop. Because others might hear? Perhaps. But what I feel is that when I form the habit of talking to myself, my desire to write decreases. When I feel that I want to write, not that I must write, things to write naturally appear before me. If I think I must write, I cannot write at all. Even as an amateur I feel this, so I can imagine the pain of people whose job is writing and who must write when they no longer want to. If talking to myself reduces the urge to write, then perhaps it is a kind of balloon effect.

There is a desire to express something. People who talk a lot release that desire through conversation. A person like me, who does not talk much, releases it through writing. Talking to myself is like sticking transparent tape to a balloon and poking it with a needle. It is not loud, but the pressure leaks out little by little. When the desire to write builds up for hours and pressure forms, I sit in front of the keyboard and have no choice but to write. But if the desire remains while the pressure is lowered enough that the writing no longer bursts out by itself, it becomes maddening. If I did not know the cause, I might have said I simply had no talent for writing. Fortunately, each time this happened I noticed that self-talk had appeared here and there. Now I treat it like alcohol or cigarettes: easy to develop as a habit, and something to avoid.

So my conclusion is that I am lucky. I like writing, and when I want to write, I can write. How could that not be luck? Of course there must be people who are talkative, sociable, good at leading conversations, and also brilliant writers. They simply are not my concern. I know only that, given my personality and circumstances, if I had loved conversation, I probably would not have written. So I am grateful for the fact that I came to like writing. It may not be the best personality for social life in Korea, and it must have cost me in various ways. But if all those disadvantages could be reversed, would I return the pleasure of writing even though I do not make a living from it? I do not think so. To unfold into words the four-dimensional image left in the mind - a set of events that occurred within a certain time - feels like making a film, if one has the ability. Anyone who can do that would try to do it at any cost.

For me, it seems only proper to think that I can endure that much disadvantage because the thing I want to do is not a costly art field, but simply writing. A money-free hobby that can be enjoyed while living an ordinary working life.

Korean original: 글쓰는 취미
