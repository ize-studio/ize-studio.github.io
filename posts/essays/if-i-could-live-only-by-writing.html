If I Could Live Only by Writing

April 29, 2024

Imagining what life will be like after I obtain something I want, or after I accomplish something, is always pleasant. Following a branch of thought, I sometimes smile alone; at other times I fall into detail and sit blankly, still happy. That is different from planning for something that will actually happen, making contingencies, and preparing for failure. Above all, these thoughts happen while I am in a good mood, so they leave my head clear.

Even within the same pleasant imagining, the mind sometimes turns in different directions. That is no problem. It is only imagination, and any direction is enjoyable.

For me, this imagination is “a life of writing only.” While working at a company, I already half-realized that life by writing as much as I want, making notes whenever thoughts come, and writing from them whenever I have time. After my wife’s advice led me to post online, I climbed one step; after trying Brunch, another. I do not know what meaning that will have. Even if I were recognized, in this fast media-driven culture that sways instantly one way and another, I do not know how long such recognition would last. I do not even know the standard by which writing is recognized. Blogs and Brunch posts I find through random searches often make me feel good, so writing something people read is a competition. I am relieved I do not have that as a goal.

I should be grateful that I can keep writing at all, and that when I want to write, things I want to turn into writing appear. It is also fortunate that I do not make my living by writing, so I can write comfortably. Even so, a life made only of writing lies at the end of my dream. If earning a living is excluded, I can feel how ideal it would be. Simply writing every day and doing nothing else looks good.

When I imagine that, I am always in Singapore. My mind skips the million-won flight ticket, nine cramped hours, immigration in halting English, the train to the lodging, and arrives directly at the hotel.

There is much to see in Singapore. More than anything, I enjoy simply walking the streets. I am not saying it is clean or orderly. Most people are walking diligently to work, so it feels like Seoul with the background changed. Staying in my room during the day in such a place reminds me, even in a strange landscape, that I am on vacation.

I spent about two weeks in Singapore on a business trip in 2023. The room was excellent for reading and writing. It was narrow, and the bathroom was so small that two people could not shower together. The room centered on the bed, and if one person stood sideways, another could not pass. Right there, against the wall, was a desk. The wall beside the bed was entirely glass, so the view came in at once. I could bury my head at the desk to write or read, turn to the right, and see the landscape in a single glance. It was possible because the room was high up, but even on a lower floor I think it would have been fun to watch people and buses.

In such a room, I would read at the desk, roll onto the bed and read, lie on my stomach and look at my phone, then sit at the desk and write on a typewriter. If something I want to write appears, I would write for three or four hours, then go down to dinner.

One might say that if I am going all that way only to stay in a hotel and read, I could do the same at home. Thinking about it, making my office or room into that sort of structure might not be bad. Singapore can remain a place I visit in imagination.

Korean original: 글만 쓰면서 살 수 있게 된다면
