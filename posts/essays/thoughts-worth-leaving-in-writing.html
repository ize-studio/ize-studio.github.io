Thoughts Worth Leaving in Writing

Sometimes while exercising, or while riding the subway and listening to music in a daze, I have a thought that seems worth leaving in writing. When that happens, I take out my phone and write a brief outline. Later, I look at that outline and write the piece again. I call it an outline, but even in the phone’s memo app it usually becomes ten or more lines. If it is shorter than that, I cannot clearly remember the flow of what I meant, and if it is longer than that, the writing begins to ignore what I had thought at the time and changes the content as it pleases.

These days, as I have been putting my phone in my bag more often while listening to music, I have been missing those thoughts more and more. It is summer, and when I sleep with the air conditioner on, I wake up cold in the early morning, or I turn it off early and wake a little early because it is hot. As that repeats, I find myself even more dazed on the way to and from work, perhaps because of sleepiness. Still, when I am on my way home after a light drink, various thoughts do sometimes come. But because that is literally something that happens only now and then, if the stop gets close while I am hesitating, I simply forget them. What is frustrating is that when I forget them that way, I still remember that there was something I had meant to write down, but only the content itself is gone. Then there is nothing I can do. The whole point of making notes on my phone was to record things somewhere close at hand, and if I miss even that, there is no possibility left. Sometimes something has suddenly come back to me in the shower, but that was when there were so many things to write while I was fully clear-headed that one or two of them could return that way. Now, when the frequency of things to write is low in the first place, once I miss one, that is the end.

If I wake up still dazed from sleep, it feels less unfair. But to be walking around perfectly awake and still lose material for writing is embarrassing. If I say I simply like writing, yet cannot write because I have nothing to write, who would look at that and think I am doing it because I like it? A hobby should be treated like a hobby, and if something is done because one likes it, it should be treated as something one likes.
