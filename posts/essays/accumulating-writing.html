Accumulating Writing

May 2, 2024

I tidy the desk simply and sit before it. Sitting at a desk itself symbolizes an intention to read or do something. Even for web browsing, lying in bed means holding a phone, but sitting at a desk forces a choice between computer and phone. Fewer things can be done only on a computer now, but some still can, so the desk remains a symbol of will.

For me, sitting at a desk means writing or reading. I use a Windows tablet, so the computer does not require a desk, but reading and writing do. I often reread books I liked rather than constantly reading new books, so perhaps I do not belong in the category of “reading a lot,” yet I insist on reading seriously at the desk. Writing is the same. If asked whether anything great comes from this, I have nothing to say. Still, to keep writing I need it. When I used notebooks, it was the same: outside I could write against a stair railing, but at home I had to sit at the desk.

Since elementary school I found writing fun, but when people who wanted to read what I wrote said it was boring, poorly written, or strange in parts, I would stop for a year or two, put it away, then take it out again after I had forgotten. If all the personal writing from twelve school years were collected, it would probably amount to only a month.

After becoming an adult and scribbling in notebooks, I never showed them, but because I never knew when or why I might stop wanting to write again, I became more afraid and more serious. It is still the same. Outside I can easily scribble on my phone, but at home that alone does not work. I may think, “Let’s write about X,” but once the sentence about X ends, I cannot continue. Yet if I touch it little by little, like coaxing a wary cat to finally lick Churu, a story forms.

If I had written happily without feedback, by now I might either have abandoned writing entirely or become someone who writes for a living. But the amount and duration of my writing are absolutely small, so I remain in a state where even I do not know it well, and where I do not see clear stylistic features when I reread. That is why I write one sentence at a time as if practicing, following thought carefully. When the ending arrives at the right moment, not too fast, I feel quietly proud and sit at the desk, not necessarily in good posture, spreading a wish on the floor that things will continue well.

I do not know how much I must write before I understand my writing. I do not know how much will let me tell myself I need not be so careful, or ignore the feeling that I am crossing a fragile glass bridge behind the pleasure. All I can do now is show my writing only to people I do not know, whose criticism need not concern me, and keep writing without expecting feedback from people I know. That is all I can think to do. It is time I knew that not stepping on my desire to write is my responsibility. Perhaps the moment I write more about things other than the simple joy of writing will be when such caution is no longer needed.

I had woken before dawn for something, gone back to sleep, and thought I had gone to work fine, but fatigue seems to have piled up more than I knew. Morning sunlight had made me happy because it felt truly like spring; by evening I had become oddly discouraged.

Korean original: 쌓여가는 글
