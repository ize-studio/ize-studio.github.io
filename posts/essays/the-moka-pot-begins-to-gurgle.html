The Moka Pot Begins to Gurgle

April 28, 2024

The moka pot began to gurgle. The distance was short, but I waited a moment because I wanted to finish the page I was reading before getting up. It was Hard-Boiled Wonderland and the End of the World. I had recently finished the second volume and was now reading the first volume I had barely managed to find. Only a few lines remained. Since I was reading a novel, not a book meant for information, rhythm mattered. Essays also need rhythm, but in fiction, things planted like hints can be missed and then cause confusion later, so one has to keep pace with the content more carefully. Usually I sit in front of the moka pot and turn it off as soon as it boils. If I am reading or looking at my phone, I get up when I hear the gurgling and slowly go turn off the stove. Today, even while hearing the sound, I read to the end of the page and the first line of the next page before slowly standing.

I turned off the gas, picked up an espresso cup and slipped my index finger through it, then lifted the moka pot by the handle. Feeling its weight, I turned back and took a cork trivet from the shelf with my other hand. It is a round trivet printed with the shape of a little water pitcher, the kind that might have been found in an old European cave. I placed it at the edge of the table, set the moka pot on it, and put the espresso cup beside it.

The sound of pouring coffee spread lightly: ttorrr. The aroma had already begun to spread as the coffee boiled, and now that it joined the sound, it truly felt like time to drink coffee.

Without opening the book again, I took a blank sip of coffee. I thought of nothing, forgot even what I had been reading, and concentrated only on the coffee. Then…

A little later, without realizing it, I stood and took out the keyboard. It was a portable mechanical keyboard. I opened the lid, placed my phone in the groove on the lid, and stood it up. The e-reader I usually use for writing was charging, so I opened the writing app on my phone and paired the keyboard. That is what makes a Bluetooth keyboard convenient. I have connection slot one set to my laptop, slot two to my phone, and slot three to my e-reader.

Sunday afternoon, a relaxed daytime, writing with a cup of coffee. I received a message from the Starbucks app saying I had earned a coupon after twelve drinks, but I will use it someday. Coffee brewed at home has its own feeling. Unlike a well-arranged cafe, I can sit any way I like, and an espresso cup feels unburdensome. Having set the atmosphere properly, I pressed the Korean consonant key a few times to check that letters appeared in the app, then began writing.

“The moka pot began to gurgle. The distance was short, but…”

Korean original: 모카포트에서 보글보글 소리가 들린다
