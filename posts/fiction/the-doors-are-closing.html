Fiction

The Doors Are Closing

October 4, 2026

The subway doors opened. With a hiss, more than half the passengers poured out like the ebbing tide. As soon as there was just enough room to move, I started weaving between the people still standing.

If I went just two cars farther, I would get off at the next station right in front of the escalator. I was at 3-1 now, and I needed to get to 5-4. Even when there are fewer people, “not crowded” during rush hour is very different from “not crowded” in the afternoon. In the afternoon, if there are no seats left, you might feel a little disappointed. During rush hour, it is enough if there is room to take a step or two should the train suddenly brake.

I took the backpack off my back, held it in one hand, and started slipping through the crowd. Then I glanced outside and realized that the door I had just passed was 4-3, and something suddenly felt strange. Everyone was still standing where they were, and now and then someone came running and got on, but even though quite a bit of time had passed, the doors still had not closed.

Well, if the train was not moving, at least it made walking easier for me.

When I reached 5-4, I understood what was going on. An old man was standing there holding a cellphone, arguing with people. He was up against one railway police officer, two police officers, and one public-service worker—four people in all.

“I was a bureau chief, you know!”

He shouted, and people turned to look at the police. I could not keep standing there in the position I was in, so I had no choice but to slowly turn and look at them too.

An old man in hiking clothes. His hair was thinning a little, but the wrinkles between his brows made him look like a man of strong will.

The railway police officer was young, though he still looked to be in his thirties.

One of the police officers looked over fifty, while the other looked as young as the public-service worker.

The public-service worker seemed to be hiding behind the railway police officer while filming with a camera that looked like a GoPro.

“Don’t touch me!”

When one of the police officers tried to take him by the arm, the old man shouted again. Then an announcement sounded inside the train.

“We have a brief announcement. This train is temporarily stopped while an incident inside the train is being dealt with. Please wait a moment. We sincerely apologize for the inconvenience.”

The incident they were dealing with had to be the one unfolding right in front of me.

Was that old man a criminal?

Then a woman’s moaning came from the old man’s cellphone. The moaning continued, followed immediately by an AI voice explaining what was happening.

“Sir, that’s a crime!”

He was reading erotic fiction with the text size turned up so large that I could read the embarrassing contents right off the screen. I had seen all kinds of stories online about people watching erotic-fiction subtitles on YouTube and things like that, but I had always just thought, So that happens, and moved on. But for a man who had just loudly announced that he had once risen all the way to bureau chief to be doing this was…

Someone shouted that what he was doing was a crime, but the old man did not lose his composure at all.

“It’s bad enough getting old and going deaf. I’m not even allowed to watch this? Is being old a crime?”

There was nothing to say. Well, that is how social life works sometimes. There are times when you have nothing to say because someone has persuaded you, and there are times when you have nothing to say because something is simply too absurd. This was the second kind.

And if it was true that he had really risen to the rank of bureau chief, I wondered what the people under him must have gone through.

“Sir, instead of doing this, please turn that off first.”

The older police officer tried to reason with him. The old man glared at him for a moment, then tapped the screen again and stopped the playback. He must have touched something by accident and started it.

“Even with ordinary radio or music, people ask you to use earphones. How can you play something like this that loudly on a subway children ride too?”

“So that makes it a crime? It would be a crime if I could hear fine and deliberately blasted it so everyone else had to listen. I’m only trying to hear it myself, and this is how loud it has to be!”

“But you can hear me just fine even though I’m speaking to you quietly, can’t you?”

“That’s different! This is a machine!”

Even though I was not the one arguing with him, listening from the side was exhausting because there was simply no getting through to him.

The older officer gave the younger officer a look, and the younger one linked arms with the old man.

“Sir, let’s not do this. Please step off the train so these people can go on their way, and you can talk with just us.”

At that, I do not know where the old man found the strength, but he violently twisted his arm, pulled free of the young officer’s grip, and shouted,

“Why should I go anywhere? Am I someone who doesn’t even have the right to ride the subway?”

Maybe he was too worked up. As he swung his arm around, his cellphone struck the young officer hard on the cheekbone. The officer’s glasses flew upward, and he crouched down holding his face without even trying to catch them.

The old man said,

“See? Why are you putting your hands on someone who was just standing here?”

But he soon fell silent, and I immediately understood why.

The older police officer’s expression had completely changed. The kind, slightly world-weary look was gone. His face had hardened, and all that remained was the stare fixed on the old man.

The old man looked for a moment as if he might answer back, but he could not withstand that gaze. He lowered his head, pressed a button, and turned off the cellphone screen, which was still displaying the embarrassing scene in a frozen frame.

Maybe thirty seconds passed like that.

The younger officer found his glasses, put them back on, and stood up again. He seemed to hesitate over whether he should take the old man by the arm again.

Then the older officer spoke.

“Cuff him. He was caught in the act.”

He immediately took out a notebook. In a completely different tone, he mechanically read out the minor-offense charges, the sexual-offense charges, and obstruction of official duties, then turned the page and read the Miranda warning as well.

His attitude made it obvious that no matter what the old man said now, he would not exchange another word with him.

Meanwhile, the younger officer handcuffed the old man. Strangely, by then the old man had completely lost his nerve. He looked nothing like he had when they had merely tried to take him by the arm.

“Take him out. Come with us.”

The words were still polite, but the older officer’s voice had gone completely flat.

Everyone silently watched.

As the three of them moved out of the train together, the public-service worker kept filming. I do not know what the old man was trying to do, but the moment he made some quick motion with his hand, the older officer took the cellphone away from him too.

Then an announcement came.

“The doors are closing. Please stand clear.”

“The platform screen doors are closing. Please stand safely back.”

The doors closed, and the train departed.
